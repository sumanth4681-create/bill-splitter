Scope · Build With AI: Basics

Bill Splitter (OCR) approved

Snap a photo of a group receipt, let each person claim what they had, and let the app do the math.

The unique kernel

Item-level claiming instead of equal splits. Each receipt item has a unique ID and can belong to one person or be shared by only the people who had it. If two people share something, the third doesn't pay for it. Anything nobody claims is split equally across the group and clearly shown.

The core loop

Who it's for

The friend who paid after a group outing. Today the group has to sit together and work out who bought what, even though everyone already knows what they had.

What "working" looks like

On a single screen, one person acts as several people. They scan a real receipt, switch between people to claim items (names appear under each item), share some items among a subset, and see each person's itemized total. Then they re-test the logic with different inputs.

The "oh, that's cool" beat: a shared item split between two people while the third pays nothing, plus unclaimed items spread across everyone, all adding up to the receipt total. The numbers below are illustrative only.

The boundary

Now: the proof of concept
  • Real OCR on a receipt photo with Google OCR, because real receipts are messy (details in spec)
  • Editable amounts to fix misreads or adjust the leftover split
  • One screen: enter the group's names, claim on behalf of each person
  • Items with unique IDs; one owner or shared by selected people
  • Everything left unclaimed split equally by default, or amounts edited by hand; shown in the summary
  • Per-person itemized summary of what each owes the payer
  • Split logic tested against varied inputs
Later
  • Shareable group link with friends on their own devices. Architecture designed now, not built or tested.
  • Accept → correct → recalculate → approval rounds → publish final split
  • Claiming by quantity for uneven splits (e.g., 3 units split 2-and-1). Even splits are already covered by subset sharing
Explicitly cut
  • In-app payments. The app shows who owes what; money moves outside it.

Why it matters to you

"Making group bill splitting easier: everyone chooses what they bought, and the app handles the calculations." It's also a chance to experiment with agent orchestration by designing the full architecture up front and building only the single-user slice.
